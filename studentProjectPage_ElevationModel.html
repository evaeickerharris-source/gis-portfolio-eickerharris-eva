Project Overview

This project demonstrates how digital elevation models (DEMs) can be used to simulate urban flooding and communicate flood risk through 3D terrain visualisation. Amsterdam was selected as the study area due to its low-lying topography, with much of the city located at or below sea level, making it particularly vulnerable to flooding. The simulation illustrates how relatively small increases in water level can dramatically expand the extent of inundation across the city, highlighting the critical role that elevation plays in flood risk. By combining terrain analysis with 3D visualisation, the project presents complex spatial information in a clear and accessible way for flood risk assessment and communication.

Methods

The project was completed in QGIS using raster-based spatial analysis and 3D visualisation techniques. Digital elevation and building datasets were processed to generate a flood depth raster, which was styled alongside hillshade and terrain models to enhance interpretation. Building height data were then incorporated into a 3D scene to create a realistic representation of the urban landscape and simulated flood extent. The final outputs included a 2D flood map, an interactive 3D visualisation, and a fly-through animation to effectively communicate the modelling results.

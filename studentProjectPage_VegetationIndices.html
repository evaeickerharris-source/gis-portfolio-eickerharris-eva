Project Overview

This project tracks vegetation loss along the Barito River in Borneo by comparing satellite-derived NDVI values between 2018 and 2025. Borneo is one of the world's most critically threatened biodiversity hotspots, with large-scale palm oil expansion driving rapid deforestation across Kalimantan. By comparing two time periods using Sentile 2 imagery processed in Google Earth Engine, this project visualises where forest cover has been lost, how severe that loss is, and how it relates to existing river and road infrastructure. The findings contribute to understanding SDG 15 (Life on Land) by making large-scale land cover change visible and spatially explicit.

Methods

Sentile 2 satellite imagery was accessed and processed through Google Earth Engine, using a median composite method to produce cloud-free images for 2018 and 2025. The study area was defined as a bounding box along the Barito River corridor in Central Kalimantan. True color composites were exported and opened in QGIS to provide a natural-colour baseline view. NDVI was calculated for both time periods using the formula (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red) and styled using a red-to-green colour ramp to represent vegetation health. A change map was then produced by subtracting the 2018 NDVI from the 2025 NDVI, revealing where vegetation cover had declined most severely over the study period.
